# The Silent Cost of Reactive HR: Why Exit Interviews Are Too Late

Every HR professional knows the feeling. A top performer schedules a sudden 1-on-1. The meeting starts with, *"I have some news..."* and ends with a two-week notice. 

You scramble to counter-offer. You conduct an exit interview. You file away the reasons—usually citing "better compensation" or "career growth." But here is the uncomfortable truth: **The exit interview is a post-mortem for a relationship that died months ago.**

## The Anatomy of Attrition

Employee departure is rarely a sudden impulse. It is a slow burn, often detectable through a combination of structural signals:

1. **The Tenure Cliff:** The statistical likelihood of departure peaks at specific intervals (e.g., 18 months, 3 years). 
2. **Compensation Ratios:** When an employee's salary falls below the 25th percentile for their role and region, flight risk spikes by 40%.
3. **Peer Attrition:** Attrition is contagious. If a manager leaves, the flight risk of their direct reports doubles within 90 days.

## Moving from Hindsight to Foresight

Traditional HR operates in the rearview mirror. We look at last quarter's turnover rate. We analyze last year's engagement survey. 

But what if you could predict who might leave next month?

This is where Workforce Intelligence Platforms come in. By combining structured HR data (HRIS, payroll) with behavioral signals and applying predictive models, organizations can identify high-risk employees *before* they start taking interviews.

### The Power of Structural Signals

Consider a mid-level engineer who hasn't had a manager check-in in 45 days, whose compensation is below market, and whose team recently lost a senior member. A predictive model flags this employee as **High Risk (85%)**, allowing the HRBP to intervene proactively.

> "Retention strategies rely on manager intuition rather than structural evidence and behavioral data. It's time to change that."

## The ROI of Predictive Retention

Replacing a highly skilled employee costs up to 200% of their annual salary. If a predictive model helps you retain just 5 key employees a year, the ROI is massive.

It's time to stop conducting post-mortems and start practicing preventative care for your workforce.
